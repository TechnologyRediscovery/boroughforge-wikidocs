# Submitting a concern

From your municipality's CodeNforce site, choose **Report a Concern**. The form walks you
through six steps, shown as a progress bar at the top of every page: **Muni → Property →
Concern → Photos → Contact → Review**.

<!-- SCREENSHOT NEEDED: The "Report a Concern" flow's step progress bar at the top of any
step page. -->

## 1. Muni

Choose your municipality. If you're not sure which one covers your address, check your local
government's website or a recent tax bill.

## 2. Property

Search for and select the property your concern is about. If your concern isn't about a
specific address — for example, a general infrastructure or patrol issue — look for the option
to continue without selecting a property.

<!-- SCREENSHOT NEEDED: The property search step showing the address search box and results
list. -->

## 3. Concern

Describe what you're reporting:

- **Concern type** — pick the category that best fits (the list is set by your municipality).
- **Description** — explain what you observed, in your own words.

<!-- SCREENSHOT NEEDED: The concern-description step with the concern type selector and
description text box. -->

## 4. Photos

Optionally attach photos supporting your concern. This step can be skipped.

<!-- SCREENSHOT NEEDED: The photo upload step showing the upload control and any already-added
thumbnails. -->

## 5. Contact

Tell us who you are:

- Choose the role that best describes you (e.g. neighbor, tenant, property owner, passerby).
- Give your **name** (required), and optionally a **phone number** and **email address**.
- **Remain anonymous?** — a toggle to request that your identity not be shared during the
  investigation. Use this if you're worried about retaliation or simply prefer not to be
  identified.
- If you'd like a copy of your submission by email, or want to receive status-update emails
  as your request moves forward, there are separate opt-in checkboxes for each — leaving both
  unchecked means you won't hear anything further by email (you can still always check status
  using your confirmation number; see
  [Checking your request status](/public/subsystems/cear/checking-status-and-unsubscribing)).

<!-- SCREENSHOT NEEDED: The Contact step showing the role selector, name/phone/email fields,
and the "remain anonymous" toggle. -->

## 6. Review

Double-check everything you entered, then submit.

<!-- SCREENSHOT NEEDED: The Review step showing a read-only summary of the whole submission
above the final submit button. -->

## After you submit

You'll see a **confirmation number** — save this. It's the only way to look up your request
later if you didn't provide an email, and it works even if you did. A code officer typically
reviews new requests within about a week.

<!-- SCREENSHOT NEEDED: The "Concern Submitted" confirmation page showing the confirmation
number box. -->

## Cautions

- **Write down your confirmation number before you leave the page** — there's no account to
  log back into and retrieve it later if you didn't also request an emailed copy.
- Status-update emails (if your municipality offers them) are entirely optional — whether you
  get any depends on both your own opt-in choice here and on whether your municipality has that
  feature turned on at all.
